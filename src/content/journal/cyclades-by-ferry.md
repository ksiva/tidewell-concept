The early boat leaves Piraeus before the city is properly awake. By the time the coffee on the upper deck has gone cold, Athens is a grey line behind you, and the first island is a shape in the haze.

Flying to the Cyclades saves hours. It also skips the part that tells you where you are. On the ferry you watch the water change colour, from harbour green to the dense blue of open sea, and you arrive the way islanders always have: slowly, from the water, with the white village climbing the hill ahead of you.

## Choose one island, then a second

The mistake is to plan five. Ferries are reliable but not frequent, and every crossing takes most of a morning. Two islands in a week is generous; one is often better.

Pair a busier island with a quiet one. Spend the first nights somewhere with restaurants and a harbour to walk, then cross to a place with one village and a handful of beaches, where the evening plan is the sunset and nothing else.

> On the ferry you arrive the way islanders always have: slowly, from the water.

## What to pack for the deck

A light jumper, even in July: the wind on the top deck is cool at speed. Water, because the café queue is long. And a printed copy of your booking, since signal comes and goes between islands.

## When to go

Late May and the second half of September are the sweet spots. The sea is warm enough, the ferries are running full timetables, and the villages belong to the people who live in them again.
